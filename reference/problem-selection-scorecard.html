Problem Selection Scorecard

A disciplined way to compare problems before falling in love with a solution.

  1. Define the user, triggering context, desired progress, and current workaround.
  2. Measure frequency, intensity, urgency, and economic or emotional cost.
  3. Seek costly behavior: money, time, risk, political capital, or repeated workaround effort.
  4. Assess reachability, budget ownership, procurement, and distribution economics.
  5. Name your information, access, capability, or timing advantage.
  6. Map assumptions by importance and evidence strength; test the riskiest first.
  7. Compare problems as a portfolio and scale only after evidence gates are crossed.
Guardrail

Complaints are not demand, interviews are not purchases, market size is not reachability, and technical novelty is not customer value.
