Decision record

Freeze what you knew and how you reasoned before reality reveals the outcome.

Before choosing

  1. Decision: What exactly must be chosen, by whom, and by when?
  2. Objective: What outcome or tradeoff matters? How will you recognize it?
  3. Alternatives: What can you do? Include “wait,” “test,” and “stop” when real.
  4. Information: What facts are available now? What remains uncertain?
  5. Base rate: What happened in comparable cases?
  6. Forecasts: Attach probabilities to the outcomes that drive the choice.
  7. Choice: Which action best fits the evidence, values, and downside?
  8. Falsifier: What observation would make you update or reverse course?
  9. Review: When will you revisit the decision?

After the outcome

  1. Record what happened without rewriting the original reasoning.
  2. Score forecasts; do not merely label the outcome good or bad.
  3. Audit process separately: frame, alternatives, information, values, reasoning, execution.
  4. Extract one update that changes a future decision rule.
Guardrail

A favorable result can follow a reckless choice; an unfavorable result can follow a sound choice. Judge a decision using information available at decision time.
