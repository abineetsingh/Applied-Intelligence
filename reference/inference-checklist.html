Inference Checklist

A compact defense against false discoveries, weak experiments, and practically irrelevant results.

  1. State whether the goal is estimation, detection, prediction, or decision.
  2. Define the null, alternative, effect size, and decision threshold before examining results.
  3. Account for false-positive cost, false-negative cost, base rate, and power.
  4. Interpret a p-value as data extremity under a model—not the probability the hypothesis is true.
  5. Report effect size and uncertainty, not significance alone.
  6. Count every outcome, subgroup, model, parameter, and stopping rule tried.
  7. Demand replication or out-of-sample confirmation when selection was flexible.
Guardrail

Statistical machinery cannot rescue a vague hypothesis, biased sample, broken measurement, or result selected after many invisible attempts.
