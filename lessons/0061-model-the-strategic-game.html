Model the strategic game

Recognize when your outcome depends on another agent's response, then represent the interaction before choosing.

Mission connection

Exceptional judgment anticipates adaptation. Game theory turns customers, competitors, partners, employees, and market participants from static assumptions into agents with incentives, information, and responses of their own.

Retrieve before reading

Reveal after answering

The decision, primitives, competing models, base rates, probabilities, causal assumptions, alternatives, downside, evidence gates, falsifiers, owners, and review dates.

The mental model

A decision is strategic when each player's best action depends on what others do. Model the players, available strategies, timing, information, outcomes, and payoffs. Payoffs must represent what actors actually value—not only money, and not what they publicly claim to value.

  1. Name the smallest set of decision-relevant players.
  2. List feasible strategies rather than vague intentions.
  3. Specify who moves when and who observes what.
  4. Estimate payoffs including risk, status, delay, and outside options.
  5. Write at least one rival model of the game.

Worked case: two SaaS competitors consider a price cut

A founder models only the revenue gained from a lower price. A strategic model also asks whether the rival will match, whether buyers will wait for further cuts, and whether both firms prefer today's margins to the likely price-war outcome. The crucial uncertainty is not demand alone; it is the rival's incentives and ability to respond.

Deliberate practice

Choose a live product, negotiation, hiring, or market decision. Write the players, strategies, move order, information, and ordinal payoffs. Then create a second model with different opponent incentives.

Deliverable: Two compact game models and the assumption that changes your action.

Transfer it

Before predicting a competitor, ask whether you have modeled their actual constraints and incentives or merely projected your own.
