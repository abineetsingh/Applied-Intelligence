Mixed case: deep pattern or resemblance?

Distinguish a transferable mechanism from a compelling surface analogy.

Mission connection

Data becomes leverage only when you understand how it was generated, how it varies, and what selection hid from view.

Retrieve before reading

What question turns an interval into a decision tool?

Reveal after answering

Would the chosen action remain acceptable across the plausible range, especially at the adverse endpoint?

The mental model

Pattern recognition transfers when the causal structure, constraints, and feedback resemble prior cases. Matching labels, shapes, or outcomes is weaker. A proposed analogy should generate a prediction that surface resemblance alone would not.

  1. State the old and new cases without metaphor.
  2. Map actors, incentives, flows, constraints, and timescales.
  3. List structural matches and structural breaks.
  4. Derive one novel prediction from the shared mechanism.
  5. Downgrade the analogy if that prediction fails.

Worked case: calling a product 'Uber for experts'

The phrase matches a two-sided interface but may miss dispatch frequency, geographic density, trust, supply utilization, regulation, and repeat behavior. If those mechanisms differ, the analogy transfers branding while hiding economics.

Deliberate practice

Choose a product or market analogy you find persuasive. Create a two-column structural map and identify the single mechanism whose absence would invalidate transfer.

Deliverable: A structural analogy audit and one falsifiable prediction.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Audit every historical market analogy by comparing policy, positioning, liquidity, valuation, and participant constraints—not chart shape alone.
