Mixed case: continue, pivot, or kill

Compare problem strength, evidence quality, reachability, advantage, and remaining test cost.

Mission connection

Founder judgment creates asymmetry by finding costly, reachable problems and buying evidence before committing to a solution.

Retrieve before reading

How can activation improve while product value worsens?

Reveal after answering

Lower friction can admit more low-intent users; activation rises while retention, value, or segment quality declines.

The mental model

A venture decision should update a portfolio, not defend an identity. Continue when critical evidence strengthens; pivot when a valuable problem remains but mechanism or segment changes; kill when core assumptions fail or superior opportunities dominate.

  1. Restate the problem without the current solution.
  2. Update each critical assumption from evidence.
  3. Compare opportunity value with alternatives.
  4. Price the next decisive evidence step.
  5. Choose continue, pivot, pause, or kill with triggers.

Worked case: high usage, no budget

Individual analysts use a tool weekly, but managers will not pay and security blocks expansion. The problem may be real while the buyer, channel, or economics fail. A pivot to a team workflow needs fresh evidence; continued feature work does not repair budget ownership.

Deliberate practice

Write an investment-committee memo for the case. Argue each option fairly, then choose based on expected opportunity value and next-test asymmetry.

Deliverable: A one-page continue/pivot/kill memo with evidence gates.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Treat product ideas as a portfolio: the relevant comparison is not idea versus nothing, but this next dollar versus the best alternative experiment.

Primary source
