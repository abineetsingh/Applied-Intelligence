Correct for repeated looks

Count every chance you gave noise to impersonate discovery.

Mission connection

Exceptional judgment distinguishes a noisy estimate from evidence strong enough to change a costly action.

Retrieve before reading

Give an example of a statistically clear effect that could still be practically irrelevant.

Reveal after answering

Any precisely measured change smaller than implementation cost or user relevance—for example, a tiny latency reduction outside a binding threshold.

The mental model

If you test enough metrics, segments, dates, models, or rules, some will look impressive by chance. Optional stopping adds more chances. The final reported result must account for the entire search, not only the winning test.

  1. Record every hypothesis, metric, subgroup, and parameter tried.
  2. Separate confirmatory tests from exploratory search.
  3. Pre-register decisions and stopping rules when possible.
  4. Correct thresholds or use held-out confirmation.
  5. Report the search budget with the winner.

Worked case: finding a winning customer segment

A team tests twenty segments and celebrates one with p = .04. Under no real differences, seeing at least one such result is unsurprising. Treat it as a hypothesis to confirm on new data, not a finished discovery.

Deliberate practice

Audit one analysis or backtest. Count all visible and invisible researcher degrees of freedom, including choices you considered but discarded.

Deliverable: A trial ledger and a clean confirmation plan.
