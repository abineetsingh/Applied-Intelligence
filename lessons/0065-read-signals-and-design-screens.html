Read signals and design screens

When information is asymmetric, distinguish cheap claims from costly signals and make hidden types reveal themselves through choices.

Mission connection

Exceptional judgment anticipates adaptation. Game theory turns customers, competitors, partners, employees, and market participants from static assumptions into agents with incentives, information, and responses of their own.

Retrieve before reading

Why should a repeated-game strategy include forgiveness?

Reveal after answering

Monitoring is noisy; without repair, an accidental or misclassified defection can trigger retaliation that destroys otherwise valuable long-run cooperation.

The mental model

A signal is informative when it is sufficiently easier or more valuable for one hidden type to produce than another. Screening reverses the direction: the less-informed party offers choices designed so different types select different options. Words alone usually pool good and bad types.

  1. Name the private information and relevant hidden types.
  2. List current signals and their costs by type.
  3. Reject signals equally cheap for imitators.
  4. Design choices that produce useful self-selection.
  5. Audit exclusion, gaming, and unintended selection.

Worked case: a startup screens design partners

Many prospects say the problem is urgent. A useful screen offers two paths: a paid pilot requiring data access and weekly operator time, or a free newsletter. Prospects with severe problems can rationally bear the first path's cost; casual interest selects the second. The commitment is evidence, though not perfect proof, of type.

Deliberate practice

Choose a hiring, sales, investing, or marketplace problem with hidden quality. Identify cheap signals, a differentially costly signal, and a self-selection screen.
