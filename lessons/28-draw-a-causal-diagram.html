Draw a causal diagram

Expose assumed mechanisms and backdoor paths before touching the data.

Mission connection

Strategic action changes systems. Causal reasoning predicts what an intervention will do, not merely what variables accompany one another.

Retrieve before reading

Why can feature usage predict retention without causing retention?

Reveal after answering

Motivation or another common cause may increase both usage and retention, creating association without an intervention effect.

The mental model

A causal diagram represents variables as nodes and direct causal assumptions as arrows. It clarifies which paths create the effect of interest, which create confounding, and which variables should not be adjusted for.

  1. Place treatment and outcome first.
  2. Add common causes of both.
  3. Add variables caused by treatment.
  4. Add selection mechanisms affecting observation.
  5. Use the graph to choose design and adjustment—not to decorate analysis.

Worked case: discounts and customer retention

Salespeople give discounts to accounts judged likely to churn. Churn risk causes discount and retention, creating confounding. Comparing discounted with full-price accounts directly may make discounts look harmful even if they help within risk levels.

Deliberate practice

Draw a five-to-eight-node causal graph for one product decision. Mark treatment, outcome, confounders, mediators, and selection variables in different colors.

Deliverable: A causal diagram plus the adjustment set you would consider.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Draw the graph behind “sentiment predicts price.” News, positioning, liquidity, and price itself may affect both measured sentiment and returns.
