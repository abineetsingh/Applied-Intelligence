Confounders, colliders, and leakage

Know when controlling for more variables creates rather than removes bias.

Mission connection

Strategic action changes systems. Causal reasoning predicts what an intervention will do, not merely what variables accompany one another.

Retrieve before reading

What belongs on a causal diagram before covariate adjustment?

Reveal after answering

Treatment, outcome, their common causes, variables caused by treatment, and mechanisms determining selection or observation.

The mental model

A confounder causes both treatment and outcome and may need adjustment. A collider is caused by two variables; conditioning on it can create a false association. Leakage lets future or outcome-derived information enter a predictor, producing impossible performance.

  1. Classify variables by causal role, not correlation strength.
  2. Adjust for pre-treatment common causes when assumptions support it.
  3. Avoid automatic control for post-treatment variables.
  4. Audit selection criteria for colliders.
  5. Enforce timestamps so future information cannot leak backward.

Worked case: analyzing only sales-qualified leads

Product fit and salesperson enthusiasm both affect qualification. Among qualified leads, low product fit may appear associated with high enthusiasm because either can secure entry. Conditioning on qualification, a collider, creates a misleading relationship.

Deliberate practice

Take your previous causal graph. For every variable you planned to control, write why it is a confounder rather than mediator or collider. Add timestamp boundaries.
