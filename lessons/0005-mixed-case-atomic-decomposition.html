Lesson 5 of 67 · 15–20 minutes

Mixed case: atomic decomposition

Integrate framing, atomization, and competing models on an unfamiliar decision.

Mission connection

Exceptional judgment starts by representing the right problem. A clean representation prevents speed from becoming fast confusion.

Retrieve before reading

Without looking back, recite the first four moves of the first-principles loop.

Reveal after answering

Specify, atomize, separate, and model. Quantify, target, and act follow in later phases.

The mental model

A mixed case tests transfer rather than recognition of yesterday's vocabulary. Work from the decision backward: only model detail that could change the choice, and resist committing to the first coherent explanation.

  1. Spend two minutes framing before analyzing.
  2. Spend four minutes producing the atomic map.
  3. Spend four minutes building two mechanisms.
  4. Spend three minutes choosing discriminating evidence.
  5. Use the final minutes to state a provisional action and confidence.

Worked case: an AI support tool with enthusiastic pilots

Five companies praise a support copilot during pilots, yet only one renews. Decide whether to keep building, change the target user, alter pricing, or stop. Possible primitives include user versus buyer, pilot subsidy, integration cost, accuracy threshold, workflow frequency, switching cost, budget owner, and measurable economic value.

Deliberate practice

Analyze the case under a 13-minute timer. Then repeat untimed and compare what the timer caused you to omit. Do not research external facts; the exercise is representation under scarcity.

Deliverable: A decision frame, atomic map, two mechanisms, one discriminating test, and a provisional choice with confidence.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Repeat this exercise on a real opportunity this week. Keep the first timed version so future lessons can measure improved speed at comparable quality.
