Make preferences and tradeoffs explicit

Replace vague priorities with explicit values, constraints, and exchange rates.

Mission connection

Insight becomes judgment only when it changes action while respecting values, downside, reversibility, and the cost of delay.

Retrieve before reading

Why can a positive expected-value action still be unacceptable?

Reveal after answering

It may violate survival constraints, have intolerable downside, rely on fragile estimates, or conflict with nonlinear preferences and opportunity costs.

The mental model

Utility represents how outcomes matter to the decision maker, not merely their dollar amount. Additional gains may have diminishing value; some thresholds are unacceptable; multiple objectives require tradeoffs. Hidden values do not disappear—they enter decisions inconsistently.

  1. List objectives separately from alternatives.
  2. Distinguish hard constraints from weighted preferences.
  3. Define direction and measurement for each objective.
  4. State tradeoffs or rank-order under realistic ranges.
  5. Test whether the preferred action changes under plausible weights.

Worked case: choosing between founder opportunities

One idea maximizes market size; another offers faster customer access, higher learning rate, and better personal fit. A single revenue estimate hides runway, motivation, control, reputation, and option value. Explicit objectives reveal which tradeoffs actually drive choice.

Deliberate practice

Create an objective hierarchy for one decision. Give each objective a measurement, unacceptable threshold, and rough relative importance.

Deliverable: An objective table and one explicit tradeoff you previously left implicit.

Transfer it

Trading objectives can include growth, maximum drawdown, liquidity, time burden, and tax consequences; return alone is not utility.
