Asymmetric upside, ruin, and irreversibility

Seek large optional upside while preventing one error from ending participation.

Mission connection

Insight becomes judgment only when it changes action while respecting values, downside, reversibility, and the cost of delay.

Retrieve before reading

What creates value in an information-gathering branch of a decision tree?

Reveal after answering

It changes the later action enough to improve expected utility, while preserving choices or delaying irreversible commitment.

The mental model

An asymmetric action has limited downside and materially larger upside, but ratios are insufficient if the downside threatens survival. Irreversible actions demand more evidence because mistakes cannot be cheaply undone.

  1. Bound direct and indirect downside.
  2. Identify ruin thresholds in money, time, trust, or health.
  3. Estimate upside magnitude and probability separately.
  4. Ask whether repeated losses remain survivable.
  5. Shrink, stage, insure, or reject irreversible exposure.

Worked case: launching a narrow paid pilot

A two-week manual pilot risks limited time and reputation while testing willingness to pay and workflow value. Building a year-long platform first risks runway and locks architecture before evidence. The pilot creates information and preserves upside with bounded exposure.

Deliberate practice

Redesign one attractive but risky opportunity into three staged commitments. State maximum loss, information gained, and scale condition at each stage.
