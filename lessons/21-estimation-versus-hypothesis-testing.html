Estimation versus hypothesis testing

Ask how large an effect is before asking whether it crossed a ritual threshold.

Mission connection

Exceptional judgment distinguishes a noisy estimate from evidence strong enough to change a costly action.

Retrieve before reading

What separates a structural analogy from surface resemblance?

Reveal after answering

Shared mechanisms and constraints produce new testable predictions; similar labels or shapes alone do not.

The mental model

Estimation asks the likely magnitude and uncertainty of a quantity. Hypothesis testing asks how compatible data are with a specified null model. Decisions usually need magnitude, downside, and cost—not only a rejection label.

  1. Name the decision-relevant quantity.
  2. Estimate its magnitude and interval.
  3. Specify a null model only if comparison helps.
  4. Define the smallest practically important effect.
  5. Choose action using consequences across plausible values.

Worked case: a pricing experiment

A price increase may produce a statistically detectable conversion decline while increasing revenue substantially. The decision needs estimated revenue, retention, and segment effects—not a binary statement that conversion changed.

Deliberate practice

Take a recent metric comparison. Rewrite the analysis as an estimation question and define the smallest effect that would alter action.
