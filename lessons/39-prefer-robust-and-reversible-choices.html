Prefer robust and reversible choices

Choose actions that remain acceptable when your estimates and models are wrong.

Mission connection

Insight becomes judgment only when it changes action while respecting values, downside, reversibility, and the cost of delay.

Retrieve before reading

What two costs must be subtracted from the expected benefit of information?

Reveal after answering

The direct cost of research and the cost of delay, including lost opportunities or changed conditions.

The mental model

A robust action performs acceptably across plausible models and parameter ranges. A reversible action can be changed cheaply after feedback. When knowledge is fragile, robustness and reversibility can dominate a slightly higher modeled optimum.

  1. List plausible models, not only parameter ranges.
  2. Evaluate each action under adverse assumptions.
  3. Identify irreversible commitments and lock-in.
  4. Prefer small probes and modular commitments.
  5. Set review triggers before switching becomes emotional.

Worked case: choosing a product architecture

A highly specialized architecture is optimal if scale and workflow assumptions are right. A simpler modular system is adequate across more futures and preserves migration options. Before product-market evidence, robustness may be worth more than theoretical efficiency.

Deliberate practice

Create a three-scenario stress test for one decision. Score each alternative on outcome, reversibility, and recovery cost if the model fails.

Deliverable: A robustness matrix and chosen review trigger.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

A strategy diversified across independent mechanisms is more robust than many parameters expressing one hidden exposure.
