Capstone: defend a consequential strategy

Integrate first principles, probability, data, causality, decision analysis, product evidence, and executive judgment.

Mission connection

Top-tier judgment recognizes when intuition is earned, finds leverage in systems, aligns incentives, and scales asymmetric bets without confusing visibility with causality.

Retrieve before reading

State the portfolio rule for creating asymmetric outcomes reliably.

Reveal after answering

Cap losses, preserve independent options, define evidence gates, kill failed theses, and concentrate resources behind validated winners.

The mental model

Exceptional judgment is not a performance of certainty. It is a traceable process that represents the right problem, quantifies uncertainty, distinguishes cause from correlation, constructs favorable exposure, and updates before reality forces it.

  1. Specify and atomize the consequential decision.
  2. Build competing models and calibrated forecasts.
  3. Audit data, causal assumptions, and missing evidence.
  4. Compare actions using utility, information, robustness, and ruin.
  5. Commit with falsifiers, owners, evidence gates, and review dates.

Worked case: your real founder, market, or life strategy

Use a decision that matters enough to expose motivated reasoning but is still within your authority. Freeze the memo before the outcome. The grade comes from representation, calibration, evidence, and process—not whether uncertainty happens to favor you once.

Deliberate practice

Write a two-page executive decision memo. Include the problem, primitives, models, base rates, probability ranges, causal assumptions, alternatives, expected utility, downside, value of information, action, and update rules. Defend it aloud in ten minutes, then invite adversarial questions.

Deliverable: A timestamped executive decision memo and a scheduled post-outcome audit.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Carry this decision memo into Phase 10: identify every actor who can adapt to your strategy, then model how their incentives may change your outcome.

Primary source

Conditions for Intuitive Expertise — Kahneman & Klein — a joint account of when intuition is likely to reflect skill rather than unsupported confidence.
