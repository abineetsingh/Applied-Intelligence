Interpret intervals

Make uncertainty visible as a range, then ask whether it changes the action.

Mission connection

Data becomes leverage only when you understand how it was generated, how it varies, and what selection hid from view.

Retrieve before reading

Why can biographies of successful executives mislead even when every fact is accurate?

Reveal after answering

The sample selects winners, omits comparable failures, and reconstructs choices after outcomes, so causal lessons are distorted.

The mental model

Point estimates imply more knowledge than samples support. An interval expresses a range compatible with the model and data. Its exact interpretation depends on method, but decision use is consistent: test actions across plausible values.

  1. Report the estimate and interval together.
  2. Name what uncertainty the interval includes and excludes.
  3. Compare the entire range with decision thresholds.
  4. Avoid turning every value inside into equally likely truth.
  5. Gather more data only if narrower uncertainty can change action.

Worked case: an experiment with modest uplift

Estimated conversion uplift is 4 points with a plausible interval from −1 to +9. If shipping is nearly free and reversible, acting may be sensible despite uncertainty. If migration is expensive and irreversible, the same interval may justify more evidence.

Deliberate practice

Take a point estimate you recently used. Construct an honest low-to-high range from sampling error, measurement uncertainty, or scenarios. Evaluate the action at both ends.

Deliverable: An interval, included uncertainties, excluded uncertainties, and threshold analysis.

Transfer it

Treat estimated trading edge as a range. Position sizing based on the optimistic endpoint is an overconfidence machine.
