Variance, tails, skew, and path

Understand why identical averages can produce survival or ruin.

Mission connection

Data becomes leverage only when you understand how it was generated, how it varies, and what selection hid from view.

Retrieve before reading

Name three distribution features an average can hide.

Reveal after answering

Spread, skew, tails, multimodality, and subgroup structure are all valid examples.

The mental model

Variance describes dispersion; skew describes asymmetry; tails describe rare extremes. Path dependence means the order of outcomes changes what remains possible. A favorable average cannot compensate for an early loss that removes you from the game.

  1. Estimate ordinary spread around the center.
  2. Inspect which tail contains the consequential extremes.
  3. Ask whether losses compound or trigger constraints.
  4. Simulate adverse ordering, not only final averages.
  5. Protect survival before maximizing expected payoff.

Worked case: two acquisition channels

Both channels average $100 acquisition cost. Channel A stays between $80 and $120; Channel B alternates between $10 and $1,000 with limited warning. If cash is constrained, Channel B's variance can end the experiment before its low-cost periods arrive.

Deliberate practice

Take one repeated decision with upside and downside. Sketch best, typical, and severe-tail outcomes, then reorder them into the worst survivable sequence.

Deliverable: A tail-risk table and one survival constraint.

Transfer it

In trading, a strategy can have positive arithmetic expectation and still be unusable because leverage and drawdown create path-dependent ruin.
