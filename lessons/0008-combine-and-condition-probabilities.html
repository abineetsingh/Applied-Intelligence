Combine and condition probabilities

Stop confusing “and,” “or,” and “given”—three words that change the calculation.

Mission connection

Limited information never disappears. Exceptional judgment makes uncertainty explicit, comparable, updateable, and accountable.

Retrieve before reading

What is the difference between aleatory uncertainty and epistemic uncertainty?

Reveal after answering

Aleatory uncertainty is variation inherent in a process; epistemic uncertainty comes from missing knowledge and may shrink with evidence.

The mental model

The complement covers what does not happen. A joint event requires multiple things together. A conditional probability restricts attention to cases where another fact is true. Most real mistakes come from using the right number for the wrong relationship.

  1. Translate the sentence into events A and B.
  2. Mark the operator: not, and, or, or given.
  3. Draw 100 hypothetical cases if notation feels abstract.
  4. Never multiply probabilities unless independence is justified or conditionals are used.
  5. Check that the result lies between zero and one.

Worked case: a product funnel

If 40% activate and 50% of activated users retain, retention among all signups is 0.40 × 0.50 = 20% only because the second number is conditional on activation. Treating 50% as an unconditional rate would misstate the funnel.

Deliberate practice

Take one funnel or trading rule and express four quantities: event, complement, joint event, and conditional event. Explain each in plain English before calculating.

Deliverable: Four event statements plus a 100-case diagram.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

For trading, distinguish P(price rises and signal fires) from P(price rises given signal fires). Only the latter begins to describe predictive usefulness.
