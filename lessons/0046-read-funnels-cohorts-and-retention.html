Read funnels, cohorts, and retention

Follow groups through time without letting changing denominators manufacture progress.

Mission connection

Founder judgment creates asymmetry by finding costly, reachable problems and buying evidence before committing to a solution.

Retrieve before reading

What makes behavioral evidence strong?

Reveal after answering

The actor sacrifices something relevant—money, time, data, workflow effort, risk, or political capital—close to the eventual desired behavior.

The mental model

Funnels describe transitions between stages; cohorts hold entry period or characteristics fixed; retention measures continued value over time. Aggregate metrics mix acquisition quality, product changes, seasonality, and maturity.

  1. Define stages with observable events.
  2. Use a stable eligible denominator.
  3. Group users by entry cohort and relevant segment.
  4. Compare curves at equal ages.
  5. Connect drop-offs to mechanisms and interventions.

Worked case: activation rises while retention falls

A simplified signup flow can push more users into activation while admitting low-intent users who churn. Aggregate active users may still rise briefly. Cohort curves and segment composition expose whether value improved.

Deliberate practice

Draw a funnel and cohort table for a product you use or imagine. Define every numerator, denominator, time window, and exclusion.

Deliverable: A metric specification and two competing explanations for one drop-off.
