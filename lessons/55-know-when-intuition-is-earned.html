Know when intuition is earned

Distinguish fast expertise from fast confidence by examining the learning environment.

Mission connection

Top-tier judgment recognizes when intuition is earned, finds leverage in systems, aligns incentives, and scales asymmetric bets without confusing visibility with causality.

Retrieve before reading

Why did the 2.1 Sharpe breakout system fail to justify capital?

Reveal after answering

Survivorship bias, same-close execution, excluded costs, extensive parameter search, and weak nearby performance contaminated the claim.

The mental model

Intuition becomes skilled when an environment has sufficiently stable cues and the decision maker receives repeated, timely, diagnostic feedback. Confidence and experience alone are not evidence. In low-validity environments, use intuition to generate hypotheses, not final probabilities.

  1. Rate environmental regularity and causal stability.
  2. Count genuinely comparable decisions experienced.
  3. Assess feedback delay, ambiguity, and selection.
  4. Check whether outcomes reveal process quality.
  5. Choose trust, verify, or override mode.

Worked case: an executive's hiring intuition

The executive has interviewed hundreds of candidates, but feedback is delayed, promotions are selected, team context changes, and rejected candidates are never observed. Experience may train confidence faster than accuracy unless outcomes and counterfactuals are tracked systematically.

Deliberate practice

Audit three domains where you trust your gut. Score regularity, repetitions, feedback speed, feedback clarity, and selection bias from 1 to 5.

Deliverable: An intuition-trust matrix with trust, verify, or override labels.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Markets provide rapid prices but not always clear causal feedback; regime change and selection can make apparent expertise unstable.

Primary source

Conditions for Intuitive Expertise — Kahneman & Klein — a joint account of when intuition is likely to reflect skill rather than unsupported confidence.
