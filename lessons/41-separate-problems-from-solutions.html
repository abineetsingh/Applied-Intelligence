Separate problems from solutions

Describe the customer's constrained progress without embedding your preferred product.

Mission connection

Founder judgment creates asymmetry by finding costly, reachable problems and buying evidence before committing to a solution.

Retrieve before reading

Why did the bounded paid pilot dominate the full build in Lesson 40?

Reveal after answering

It tested costly behavior and workflow, limited irreversible investment, and preserved the later option to scale.

The mental model

A problem exists in a user's context before your solution. Describe actor, trigger, desired progress, obstacle, consequence, and current workaround. Solution language narrows observation and invites confirmation bias.

  1. Name a specific user in a specific context.
  2. Describe the triggering event and desired progress.
  3. Identify constraints and consequences of failure.
  4. Observe current workarounds and alternatives.
  5. Write the problem without naming your technology.

Worked case: an AI meeting-summary idea

“Teams need AI summaries” embeds a solution. A stronger thesis is: “After cross-functional customer calls, account owners spend 45 minutes reconciling commitments across tools, and missed handoffs delay deals.” This can be solved, measured, or disproven in multiple ways.

Deliberate practice

Write three solution-free problem statements from opportunities you are considering. Ban product categories, features, and technologies from the wording.

Deliverable: Three problem theses containing user, context, progress, obstacle, consequence, and workaround.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Separating problem from solution preserves strategic flexibility: the best intervention may be workflow, service, integration, policy, or software.
