Interpret p-values correctly

Read a p-value as a conditional statement about data, not a verdict about truth.

Mission connection

Exceptional judgment distinguishes a noisy estimate from evidence strong enough to change a costly action.

Retrieve before reading

What determines whether false positives or false negatives deserve more protection?

Reveal after answering

Their decision consequences, reversibility, base rates, and the value of the effects that could be missed.

The mental model

A p-value is the probability, assuming the null model and analysis are valid, of obtaining data at least as incompatible with that model as what was observed. It is not the probability the null is true, the probability results arose by chance, or the effect's importance.

  1. State the null model precisely.
  2. Verify the analysis was not selected after seeing data.
  3. Read p as P(data this extreme | null), not P(null | data).
  4. Pair p with effect size and interval.
  5. Return to the decision and error costs.

Worked case: a p-value of 0.03

Under the specified null and procedure, results this extreme or more would occur about 3% of the time. This does not mean a 97% chance the product change works. Prior plausibility, design quality, multiplicity, magnitude, and costs remain.

Deliberate practice

Find or invent a p-value claim. Write three incorrect interpretations and one correct interpretation, then add the missing decision information.

Deliverable: A corrected p-value statement plus effect size and decision threshold.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Treat a strategy's small p-value cautiously when the rule, assets, horizon, or sample was chosen after inspecting results.
