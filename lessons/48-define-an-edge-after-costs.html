Define an edge after costs

Turn a market story into a forecast, benchmark, and executable payoff distribution.

Mission connection

Market judgment demands falsifiable edges, honest chronology, realistic costs, survival-focused sizing, and resistance to backtest seduction.

Retrieve before reading

What distinguishes pivoting from continuing or killing?

Reveal after answering

A pivot preserves validated learning about a real problem while changing a failed segment, channel, solution, or economic mechanism.

The mental model

An edge is positive expected value relative to a relevant benchmark after all implementation costs and constraints. A pattern is not an edge until it specifies what happens, over what horizon, how it is traded, and what comparison it beats.

  1. Define signal using only observable contemporaneous data.
  2. Specify entry, exit, horizon, and sizing.
  3. Choose a benchmark reflecting available alternatives.
  4. Include execution and financing costs.
  5. State the forecast and falsification threshold.

Worked case: momentum appears strong

“Buy strong charts” is not testable. A claim might be: among liquid U.S. equities, rank twelve-month returns excluding the latest month, hold the top decile for one month, rebalance monthly, and exceed a specified benchmark after estimated turnover costs.

Deliberate practice

Translate one trading belief into a complete strategy specification without adjectives such as strong, clean, or oversold unless numerically defined.

Deliverable: A falsifiable edge statement with benchmark and cost model.

Transfer it

Use the same discipline for product growth claims: define intervention, outcome, horizon, benchmark, and full cost.
