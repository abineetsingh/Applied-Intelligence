Lesson 1 of 67 · 15–20 minutes

Separate the decision from the outcome

Your first upgrade is to stop letting luck grade your reasoning.

Why this matters

Founders and traders receive noisy feedback. If you copy every lucky action and abandon every unlucky one, experience can make your judgment worse.

The distinction

A decision is the choice produced by a process using information available at the time. An outcome is what later happened, including factors you could not control.

This creates four possibilities:

Good outcomeBad outcome
Sound processEarned successUnlucky loss
Weak processLucky escapeExpected failure

The purpose is not to excuse failure. It is to extract the right lesson. A single result gives weak evidence about a probabilistic process; repeated, well-recorded results give much stronger evidence.

Retrieval check

Classify both the process and the result. Do not let the result determine the process label.

Use it on a live decision

Choose a real decision you have not resolved yet. Keep it small enough to revisit within a few weeks. Recording it now prevents hindsight from changing what you remember knowing.

Finish the lesson

  1. Copy the decision record.
  2. Paste it into your teacher chat for feedback.
  3. Schedule the review date you named.
Primary source

The Decision Education Foundation explains why process quality and outcome quality must be judged separately and offers a six-part decision-quality framework. Read “Defining Decision Quality.”

If any distinction feels slippery—or your real decision does not fit the template—ask your teacher a follow-up question. Edge cases are where the useful learning begins.
