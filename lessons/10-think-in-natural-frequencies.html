Think in natural frequencies

Convert conditional percentages into countable cases your mind can inspect.

Mission connection

Limited information never disappears. Exceptional judgment makes uncertainty explicit, comparable, updateable, and accountable.

Retrieve before reading

What is the outside view, and what should happen before case-specific adjustment?

Reveal after answering

It starts from outcomes in a causally relevant reference class; the class rate becomes the prior before documented adjustments.

The mental model

Conditional percentages hide denominators. Natural frequencies express the same information as counts that preserve the sampling structure: out of 1,000 cases, how many belong to each branch? This often makes Bayesian reasoning almost mechanical.

  1. Choose a convenient population such as 1,000 cases.
  2. Apply the base rate first.
  3. Split true cases by sensitivity or hit rate.
  4. Split false cases by false-positive rate.
  5. Compare true hits with all observed hits.

Worked case: a noisy fraud alert

Suppose 1% of 1,000 transactions are fraudulent: 10 frauds. A system catches 90%, yielding 9 true alerts. If it falsely flags 5% of 990 legitimate transactions, that adds about 50 false alerts. Only about 9 of 59 alerts indicate fraud—roughly 15%, not 90%.

Deliberate practice

Build a 1,000-case frequency tree for a product lead score, medical test, or trading signal. Use invented but clearly labeled assumptions if real rates are unavailable.

Deliverable: A frequency tree showing true hits, misses, false alarms, and correct rejections.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Any rare chart pattern needs this treatment: start with how often the desired move occurs without the pattern, then count true and false signals.
