Reason with distributions

Replace the typical case with shape, spread, and meaningful subgroups.

Mission connection

Data becomes leverage only when you understand how it was generated, how it varies, and what selection hid from view.

Retrieve before reading

Distinguish a parameter from an estimate in one sentence.

Reveal after answering

A parameter is an unknown property of the target population; an estimate is the sample-derived approximation of it.

The mental model

An average compresses a distribution and discards information. Decision quality may depend on dispersion, skew, multimodality, or subgroup differences. Always ask what different distributions could share the same mean.

  1. Plot or imagine the full distribution before summarizing.
  2. Locate center with mean and median.
  3. Measure spread and inspect tails.
  4. Look for clusters caused by mixed populations.
  5. Choose summaries aligned with the decision's loss function.

Worked case: average customer value

Average annual revenue of $10,000 could describe uniform accounts near $10,000 or a few $500,000 accounts plus many near zero. The first supports a standardized motion; the second demands segment economics and concentration risk.

Deliberate practice

Create two imaginary ten-value datasets with the same mean but opposite business implications. Compute median and range, then state which decision each supports.

Deliverable: Two distributions, three summaries, and contrasting decisions.

Check your judgment

Answer before rereading. Feedback appears immediately.

Transfer it

Compare strategies using return distributions and drawdowns, not average return alone.
