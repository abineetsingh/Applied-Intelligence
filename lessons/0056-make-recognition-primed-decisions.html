Make recognition-primed decisions

Use experience to generate a plausible action, then simulate it before execution.

Mission connection

Top-tier judgment recognizes when intuition is earned, finds leverage in systems, aligns incentives, and scales asymmetric bets without confusing visibility with causality.

Retrieve before reading

What two environmental conditions support skilled intuition?

Reveal after answering

Learnable regularities and sufficient opportunities for timely, diagnostic feedback from genuinely comparable decisions.

The mental model

Experienced practitioners often recognize a situation and retrieve the first workable action rather than compare every option. The discipline is to diagnose anomalies and mentally simulate the candidate through likely consequences before acting.

  1. Recognize the situation prototype and relevant cues.
  2. Generate the first plausible action.
  3. Mentally simulate its sequence and side effects.
  4. Look for expectancies violated by current facts.
  5. Act, modify, or switch to deliberate comparison.

Worked case: a production incident under time pressure

An experienced engineer recognizes database saturation and considers shedding load. Before acting, she simulates customer impact, checks whether queue growth and replica lag match the pattern, and notices an anomaly suggesting a retry storm. Recognition accelerates diagnosis; anomaly checking prevents rote response.

Deliberate practice

Choose a familiar domain and reconstruct one fast judgment. List cues noticed, prototype retrieved, candidate action, mental simulation, and anomaly that would force slower reasoning.
